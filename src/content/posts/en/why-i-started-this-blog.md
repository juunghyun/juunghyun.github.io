Nobody works without an LLM anymore. People hand a pricey model the whole job and let it run. And honestly, it's worth the money. Even juniors can now take on the work they always wanted to do.

But there's a mix now: people who know what's going on under the hood, and people who don't. Maybe everyone's career stopped growing the day they started using AI. The experience you're supposed to pick up on the way to becoming an expert in something, we handed to the AI along with our dollars. I have a feeling that how fast I get work done with AI and how fast I actually grow might be inversely related.

There's a limit to how much a person can take in, and a top speed to how fast we learn. Anything faster than that just doesn't stick. That's the biggest pain point I've felt.

So here's the plan. I'll keep doing the work, but I'll write down the insights I get from it myself. No help from anything else. I want to share what I learned by putting it into my own words.

I'm not saying don't use AI. Skim the recent blogs of developers at big tech companies, here in Korea and abroad, and more than half of it is about AX, AI transformation. What AX is, what AI is, and how I should look at all of it, that's something I need to keep thinking about. Honestly, this is less advice for anyone else and more a note to myself.

This won't be "I tried some new tech." It'll be the problems I actually ran into and how I solved them. I'm writing them down to make them mine. And if you, the visitor, walk away with something too, even better.
